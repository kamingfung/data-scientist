# Executive Presentations & Punchy Summaries

Design decks and executive summaries top-down so a busy decision-maker gets the takeaway in under 30 seconds. See [references/slides-and-qa.md](references/slides-and-qa.md) for table transforms, 2x2 matrices, and Q&A scripts.

## 1. Deck Architecture (Fractal Top-Down)

- **Write the "Final Slide" first:** place a single decision scorecard upfront comparing options against quantified criteria before diving into detail.
- **Title every slide with a "So What?" conclusion:** state the takeaway (`"Midwest margin fell 400 bps from freight mix"`), never a topic label (`"Midwest Margin Analysis"`).
- **Use the fractal 3-point structure:** open with the main conclusion, prove it with at most three supporting points, then restate the conclusion and invite Phase 2 questions.
- **Ship 100% complete at 50% detail:** finalize the full storyline and conclusion titles before polishing low-level charts or appendix tables.
- **Cap slide density:** keep decks to `<=12` slides (move deep dives to the appendix), `<=4` bullets per slide, and `<=5–6` words per bullet.

## 2. Visuals & Delivery

- **Replace raw tables with derived visuals:** convert raw numbers into `% growth`, `% share`, or a `2x2` matrix so patterns read at a glance.
- **Keep slides readable in grayscale:** limit categorical colors to `<=4`, pair color with labels or markers, and avoid red–green pairings.
- **Apply the Scorsese Principle:** pair key claims with one concrete analogy, visual, or short story that makes the stakes memorable.
- **Never read slides verbatim:** use slides as visual proof while delivering conversational spoken transitions.

## 3. Punchy Summaries & Q&A

- **Headline Principle:** open every summary, email, or spoken answer with the single most important takeaway first.
- **Pasta-Sauce Principle:** reduce drafts by ~25% by cutting preamble, throat-clearing, and chronological recaps (`"First we checked X, then Y"`).
- **Conviction Principle:** eliminate hedge words (`"kind of"`, `"sort of"`, `"just"`, `"I think"`, `"maybe"`) and state conclusions directly.
- **No-Tailgating Principle:** pause deliberately after delivering a key number or recommendation so the audience absorbs it.
